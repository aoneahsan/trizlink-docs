Hootsuite is the large enterprise suite, priced per user, with inbox automation, listening and an AI assistant. A team running customer care from the inbox with automated routing, or tracking conversations across social, news and AI answers, needs what Hootsuite sells. If ninety-nine dollars a user a month is more than the work needs, and the bio page and short links should be first-class rather than a sub-feature, Trizlink is the smaller tool. [Trizlink and Hootsuite](https://trizlink.com/compare/hootsuite).

## Taking the links out of Hootsuite {#steps}

**Hootsuite stays in charge of publishing, the inbox and listening.** Trizlink's publishing is built and switched off; [that page](/features/social-media#not-live) says why.

1. **List the seats that only ever touched links.** People who made campaign links and read click reports do not need a Hootsuite seat for that.
2. **[Create a Trizlink workspace](/getting-started/create-account)** and invite them as members, or viewers if they only read numbers. [Roles](/features/workspaces-and-teams#roles) come with every plan.
3. **Make links in Trizlink rather than relying on Hootsuite's automatic shortening.** Each link gets its own report, its own UTM defaults and, if you need it, a password or an expiry date.
4. **Save your campaign tags as [UTM templates](/features/tracking-and-utm#utm)** so every person tags the same way.

## What stays on Hootsuite {#stays}

- **The inbox, listening and automated routing.**
- **Scheduled posts, connected accounts and history.**
- **Approvals.** Trizlink has none.
- **TikTok, YouTube and Pinterest.** Not connectable here.
